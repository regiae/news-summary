Reuters reported that US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine entered a second day in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, the first direct negotiations in nine months. President Volodymyr Zelensky said a prisoner exchange of 1,000 for 1,000 was discussed, while Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov ruled out a ceasefire before a wider security deal.

A city bus crashed into 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, on Wednesday morning, injuring 57 people, nine of them seriously. Yonhap reported that the driver said the brakes had failed.

Bulgaria observed a national day of mourning on Wednesday for the 12 people killed in a pile-up of about 86 vehicles on the Trakia motorway the day before. The AP reported that dense fog was blamed for the crash, which also injured dozens more.

World leaders opened a two-day summit at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on Wednesday, aiming to turn climate pledges into concrete action. TBS News reported the talks would focus on clean energy and climate finance, amid warnings that efforts remain too slow.

The 990-foot ocean liner SS United States was sunk off Destin, Florida, on Wednesday to become the world's largest artificial reef, a day earlier than planned because of forecast high winds. The Pensacola News Journal reported the sinking began at 1 p.m. local time, about 22 nautical miles offshore.

TSMC executive Kevin Zhang told the Nikkei Forum that demand for the chipmaker's most advanced silicon was 'insatiable', with AI customers seeking all the computing power they could get. Nikkei Asia reported the remarks.

Asian shares rose on Wednesday, the AP reported, with Tokyo's Nikkei 225 up 1.3% to 50,947.59 and Seoul's Kospi up 2.4% to 4,197.71.
