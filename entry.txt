US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine entered a second day in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, the first direct negotiations in nine months, Reuters reported. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the first day very productive, but there was no sign of a breakthrough, with the sides far apart.

Oil prices rose on Wednesday as attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz continued, Reuters reported, with Brent crude up 0.8% to $92.20 a barrel. Thirty-seven ships were attacked there in October, the most in a month since the war began, and 10 more in November.

Asian shares rebounded on Wednesday, investinglive reported, with Tokyo's Nikkei 225 up 0.8% to about 60,940 and Seoul's Kospi up 1.7% to about 5,330, led by chip stocks. South Korea's Kosdaq jumped about 5%, while SoftBank fell on a quarterly loss of about 1.1 trillion yen.

TSMC said its board had approved up to $500 million each in Anthropic and OpenAI, Morningstar reported, a $1 billion bet on the AI firms driving demand for its chips. Anthropic set its IPO range at $70 to $78 a share, valuing it at up to $1.4 trillion.

UN chief Antonio Guterres warned leaders opening a two-day summit at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya on Wednesday that the slow response to climate change had put the world on a path to destruction, Reuters reported. Nearly 200 countries are at the talks, aiming to turn climate pledges into action on clean energy and finance.

The US House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, shelving the effort for this Congress, IANS reported. They accused him of waging war in Iran without congressional approval.

The 990-foot liner SS United States was sunk off Destin, Florida, on Wednesday to become the world's largest artificial reef, the Pensacola News Journal reported, a day early because of forecast high winds. Scientists will study how marine life colonises the wreck.
