Russia and Ukraine agreed a large-scale prisoner exchange as US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended on Wednesday, Reuters reported. Envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of the war; he and Steve Witkoff will meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow.

Brent crude rose 0.6% to $92.01 a barrel on Wednesday as shipping attacks in the Strait of Hormuz continued, Reuters reported. The Washington Institute counted 37 vessels attacked there in October, the highest monthly total of the war, and 10 more in November.

Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, the Associated Press reported, with 11,042 infected and 5,387 dead, its deadliest outbreak. The Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine and has reached eight of Congo's 26 provinces.

World leaders opened a two-day COP31 summit in Antalya, Reuters reported, with 102 heads of state expected. UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow response to climate change had put the world on a path to destruction.

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported; core prices rose 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast, the last reading before the Fed meets on December 8-9.

The US House voted 211-204 to send eight impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, NPR reported, ending the effort. Its author, Republican Thomas Massie, was the only Republican to vote against it.

A city bus plowed into 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people, Yonhap reported; nine were seriously hurt and the driver told police the brakes had failed.
